About the project

Most language apps teach translation: a word next to a picture. You memorize the match, but you don't learn to act in the language.

Philosophy of language

Ludwig Wittgenstein: meaning is use

Philosophical Investigations (1953): a word means what it does inside a "language game". So no picture translates a phrase, every word appears in at least two different situations, and a mistake is explained by the character's reaction, not punished.

This matches research on active recall (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006) and "desirable difficulties" (Bjork).

Photo: Ludwig Wittgenstein, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.

Screenwriting

Blake Snyder: Save the Cat

Save the Cat! (2005): a proven 15-beat Hollywood structure gives the player a goal and stakes. You speak because the hero needs it, not because an exercise says so.

Photo: savethecat.com/bio.

Why it works: the story keeps motivation up, the language games build understanding instead of rote memory. You leave able to use words in the situation they were made for.
